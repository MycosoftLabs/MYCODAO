## The front of the pipeline

Most new fungal records come from people in the field: amateur mycological societies, foragers, park staff and students. Their observations decide which species the rest of the pipeline can study.

## What makes a find useful

- A clear **determination**: which characters you saw (cap, gills or pores, stipe, spore print, smell, substrate).
- **Evidence**: photos, a spore print, or a dried voucher.
- **Context**: substrate, habit, date, and location at a precision that protects sensitive sites.
- **Confidence**: tentative, supported by macroscopic characters, or confirmed by microscopy or sequencing.

## How discoverers get paid

- **Species rewards (beta, devnet).** Approved observations of known species can earn that species' token. Eligible new or rare records can earn MYCO under the funded reward policy.
- **Bounties (planned).** Projects post needs, such as "salt-tolerant oyster isolates from coastal San Diego," with a stated payout for accepted specimens.
- **Tissue contributions (planned).** A specimen that yields a viable culture in the biobank can earn an additional reward once the culture is accessioned.

Rewards depend on review, an eligible policy and allocated funds. Submitting an observation does not guarantee a reward.

## Provenance is value

A culture is only as useful as its record. Without who, where, when and how it was collected, a specimen cannot be used in research, sold, or licensed with confidence.

## Record at collection

- Collector name and date.
- Location, kept at full precision privately and published only at county level or coarser for sensitive species.
- Substrate and host (wood species, soil, dung, living plant).
- Photos *in situ* before picking.
- A collection number written on the bag and in the record.

## Permits and permission

- **Land permission.** Public lands, parks and private property all have their own rules. Many parks prohibit collection without a research permit.
- **Protected species.** Some fungi and lichens are legally protected.
- **International material.** Under the **Nagoya Protocol** (in force since 2014), using genetic resources from another country usually requires *prior informed consent* and *mutually agreed terms* that share the benefits with the providing country.

FungIP records these permissions next to each specimen, so later licensing never depends on a specimen with unclear origins.

## Isolating a culture

1. Take a small piece of internal tissue from a fresh, clean fruiting body (the inside of the stipe or cap is cleanest).
2. Place it on sterile agar in a still-air box or flow hood.
3. Transfer clean edges of new mycelium to fresh plates until the culture is free of contaminants.

## Storage forms

The biobank records the *form* of every holding: agar plates, slants, liquid culture, grain spawn, dried specimens in jars, and cryopreserved samples. Each form has a different shelf life and replating schedule.

## Status and health

- **Status** tracks where the holding is: stored, reserved, transferred.
- **Health** tracks whether it is viable: healthy, contaminated, or not yet checked.
- **Replates due** lists cultures that need to be refreshed to stay alive.

## Why this matters for the economy

A living, documented culture is what turns a field observation into something a lab can study or a company can license. The Tissue Catalog's digital twin mirrors the physical vault, so each accession can be inspected, along with its history and photos.

## What MINDEX holds

MINDEX is Mycosoft's species database. For each fungal taxon it stores:

- accepted name and full lineage (kingdom to species)
- common names
- reference images with license and attribution
- collection ids and sequence accessions where available

## How Blocks uses it

- **Observations** are filed against a MINDEX taxon id, so every reward points at the same species record.
- **The Tissue Catalog** links physical holdings to their MINDEX taxon.
- **Species tokens** are created per MINDEX taxon, so one species has one token.

## Licensed reference, not ownership

MycoDAO displays MINDEX records under a licence from Mycosoft. A species record is reference information. It does not make anyone the owner of the species or its DNA.

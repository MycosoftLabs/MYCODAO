## Why fungi need an economy

Scientists have described roughly 150,000 fungal species. Estimates of how many exist range from about 2.2 to 3.8 million, which means more than 90% of fungi are still unknown to science. Every undescribed species is a potential source of new materials, medicines and enzymes, and almost none of them are being looked for systematically.

Discovery is slow because the people who find fungi — foragers, amateur mycologists, field technicians — are rarely paid for what they find, and the value a discovery creates later (a drug lead, a material, a dataset) almost never flows back to them.

## The loop MycoDAO is building

1. **Discover.** Foragers and mycologists find and record fungi in the field.
2. **Record.** Each find becomes a dated observation with location precision, photos and a determination, filed against a MINDEX taxon.
3. **Preserve.** Specimens and living tissue go into the biobank, where cultures are isolated, stored and replated.
4. **Study.** Researchers sequence cultures, screen them for compounds, and test materials.
5. **Make.** Validated materials, medicines and services are developed with partners under written agreements.
6. **Pay back.** Revenue and grants fund the treasury, which pays rewards and bounties for the next round of discovery.

Each turn of the loop makes the catalog larger and more valuable, which in turn funds more discovery.

## What exists today and what is planned

- **Live:** the Tissue Catalog and biobank records, the MINDEX species library, species rewards on Solana *devnet*, and MycoDAO governance in Realms.
- **Planned:** paid biobank services, MYCO-funded project needs, licensing under named agreements, and revenue flowing back to discoverers.

Throughout these lessons, anything that is not yet running is labeled *planned*. Holding MYCO today gives no royalty, revenue or ownership rights.

## A DAO for fungal science

A decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) is a group that coordinates money and decisions through on-chain rules instead of a single company board. MycoDAO's purpose is narrow: discover fungi, follow the science, and help fund what comes next.

## Where decisions happen

MycoDAO's governance lives in a **Realm** on Solana (Realms is the standard governance app for Solana DAOs). A proposal can request treasury funds, change a policy, or approve a program. MYCO holders vote, and the result is recorded on-chain.

## What governance covers

- Approving funding for documented project needs (for example, a sequencing batch for Project Oyster).
- Setting reward policies for species observations.
- Approving partnerships and the terms under which materials or data are shared.

## What it does not do

A vote does not create legal rights in a species, and it does not replace contracts. Licensing a material or an invention still needs a written agreement with a named rights holder. The DAO decides *whether* to pursue something; agreements define *how*.

## Current status

The Realm exists and proposals can be viewed. Recorded votes and the exact voting rules still need to be verified and published. Check the MYCO tab for the current stage of each MYCO use.

## Why stages matter

Science funding goes wrong when goals are presented as results. On Blocks every milestone, funding need and MYCO use carries a stage label so you can see exactly what has happened.

## Milestone states

- **Planned** — the step is defined but has not started.
- **In progress** — work is underway.
- **Completed with evidence** — the step is done *and* a record is attached (a dataset, accession, report, permit or payment).
- **Delayed** — the step is late; the reason should be stated.
- **Unverified** — someone says it happened, but no record is attached yet.

## Funding stages

Money moves through **target → approved budget → reserved → committed → paid**. A target is arithmetic, not money. Only *paid* means funds actually reached a recipient.

## How to read a project page

1. Look at which milestones have evidence attached.
2. Check the funding stage, not just the amount.
3. Follow the evidence link and see whether it supports the claim.

If a page says something is complete without a record, treat it as unverified, and tell the project owner.
